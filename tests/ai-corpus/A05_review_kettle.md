# Review: The Fellow Stagg EKG Electric Kettle

I have used the Stagg EKG daily for six months, mostly for pour-over coffee and the occasional pot of tea. Here is how it has held up.

## Design and build

The Stagg is undeniably beautiful. The gooseneck spout, the matte finish, and the minimalist base give it a presence that most kitchen appliances lack. It feels solid in the hand, and the counterbalanced handle makes controlled pouring effortless. This is a kettle designed by people who care about the ritual of making coffee, and it shows.

## Performance

Temperature control is the headline feature, and it delivers. You can set any temperature between 135 and 212 degrees Fahrenheit in one-degree increments, and the kettle holds it within a degree or two. For pour-over, being able to dial in 200 degrees for a light roast and 205 for a darker one makes a real difference in the cup. The hold function keeps water at temperature for up to an hour, which is convenient on slow weekend mornings.

Boiling speed is adequate rather than fast. A full 0.9-liter fill takes about five minutes to reach boiling, slower than a conventional kettle of similar capacity.

## What could be better

The capacity is small. At 0.9 liters, it is enough for two large cups but not for a household of tea drinkers. The base is also somewhat bulky, and the power cord is short. Finally, the price is high for a kettle, even a very good one.

## Who it is for

If you make pour-over coffee regularly and care about temperature precision, the Stagg EKG is one of the best tools you can buy. If you mainly want to boil water quickly for a large family, a conventional kettle will serve you better for a fraction of the price.

## Verdict

The Stagg EKG is a specialist tool that does its one job exceptionally well. It is expensive, it is small, and it is slow to boil, but for the right person, none of that matters. It has earned its place on my counter.
