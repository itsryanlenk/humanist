I spent the last year learning to code in public, posting every small win and every embarrassing bug. Here is what I learned about learning.

1. The fear of looking stupid is the biggest obstacle, and it is entirely self-imposed. Nobody is judging your beginner code as harshly as you are. Most people are too busy worrying about their own.

2. Writing about what you learned is the fastest way to find out you did not actually learn it. Every time I tried to explain a concept in a post, I discovered a gap in my understanding. The post became the study session.

3. Consistency beats intensity. Posting one small thing every day did more for my skills than the weekends I spent grinding through tutorials for eight hours straight. Small, steady, visible progress compounds.

4. The community shows up when you do. My first ten posts got almost no engagement. By post fifty, experienced developers were replying with corrections, resources, and encouragement. You have to earn the audience by showing up before it exists.

5. Documenting the journey creates a record you will actually use. I have gone back to my own posts dozens of times to remember how I solved something. Future me is the most grateful reader.

The hardest part was the first post. Everything after that was just showing up again.

If you are thinking about learning something in public, start today. Start small. Start before you feel ready. The readiness comes from the doing, never the other way around.
